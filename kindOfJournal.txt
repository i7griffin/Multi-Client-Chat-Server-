Thread-per-client (what I built)	
It is simple to write, natural shared-memory broadcasting, good for small-to-moderate scale
Doesn't scale — real memory/thread cost per client, breaks down around hundreds-to-thousands of connections



Process-per-client	
Strong isolation (a crash in one client can't corrupt another)	
Broadcasting requires extra IPC machinery — the exact feature this server centers on becomes hard


Thread pool	
Bounds resource usage, predictable	
Idle clients can starve capacity from active ones, since a worker stays occupied by a client even when that client is doing nothing



Event-driven (epoll)	
Scales to tens of thousands of connections on one thread, since idle connections cost almost nothing
More complex code — no natural "blocking wait for this one client" mental model; requires rethinking the whole I/O structure
